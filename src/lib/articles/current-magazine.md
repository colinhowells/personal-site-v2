Since I was a kid my dream was to work as a print designer, ideally on magazines. The dream came true, and for nearly seven years I was one of the designers for _[Current](https://www.ecurrent.com),_ an ad-supported free monthly arts-and-entertainment magazine in Ann Arbor, with a circulation of roughly 20,000. It was well regarded in town; the editorial department did a terrific job keeping up on local events, writing features and maintaining a stable of columnists.

When I started, the desktop-publishing revolution had yet to happen – we would assemble materials we’d shot with stat cameras in-house on pasteboards using hot wax, which would then be shot by the printer. I worked on creating a modern PDF workflow in which all the layout work would take place in computers (an assortment of PowerPC Macs), PDFs would be generated through Adobe Distiller, and then emailed to the printer. We didn’t get proofs due to our working close to deadlines, so we had to be very careful with our processes to make sure the printer could successfully handle whatever we provided.

While moving to a fully-digitized workflow I also redesigned the magazine, which was something I’d been excited to do ever since leaving college. I think at the time I was influenced by alt-weeklies like the _Chicago Reader_ and _The Stranger._

Often our cover photography wasn’t related to any features, but sometimes we got lucky and got something great to work with, as we see below in the December 2003 cover from the illustrious [Doug Coombe](http://www.dougcoombe.com) who did great work for Detroit’s _MetroTimes_ and went on to shoot for _Rolling Stone._
